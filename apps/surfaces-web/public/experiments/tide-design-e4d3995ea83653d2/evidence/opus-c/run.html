Tide prototype — run opus-c (NN/g UX resource)

Outcome

  • One-screen responsive tide app in src/App.jsx + src/styles.css, built to dist/ (index.html, app.js, app.css, unchanged data.js).
  • Controls: location <select> (14 stations, alphabetical) and a two-button day toggle (Tue Oct 6 / Wed Oct 7). Initial: Half Moon Bay, 2026-10-06.
  • Readings: noon height in large type with a Rising/Falling pill; next high and next low cards (time, height, hours from noon), sorted by which comes first; 24-hour SVG line chart with a labelled metre axis, 3-hour time ticks (6-hour ticks on narrow screens), a noon marker, and value labels on each high and low; a collapsible table of hourly heights.
  • Units, timezone and clock are stated in the header ("Heights in metres (m) · Times in Pacific Daylight Time (PDT)"), in the reading line ("as of 12:00 PM (noon) PDT"), and in the chart subtitle.
  • All values come straight from public/data.js (currentHeight, trend, nextHigh, nextLow, events, samples). Nothing is calculated except the chart scale and time formatting.
  • Light mode only. No demo banner. No live APIs and no web fonts.

Evidence applied (corpus claim → my decision)

  • Corpus: Data labels make exact values easy to read. Axes and grid lines are worth keeping when the general magnitude matters. (Kate Moran, "Clutter-Free: One of the 3 Cs for Better Charts", https://www.nngroup.com/articles/clutter-charts/, section "Axes".) Inference/decision: Keep light grid lines and a metre y-axis so the overall tide shape and range are readable. Add exact value labels only on highs and lows, which are the values people look for.
  • Corpus: Scale charts to show the range of values accurately. A zero baseline is natural in many cases, but you don't have to force it when another logical baseline exists. (Lexie Kane, "Designing Effective Infographics", https://www.nngroup.com/articles/designing-effective-infographics/, section "Informational Honesty".) Decision: The y-axis starts at 0 m, which is the fixture's natural baseline because every height is positive. The top is the selected day's maximum plus at least 0.2 m, rounded up to 0.5 m. The scale changes with each selection and always contains the data.
  • Corpus: 2D position, as used in line graphs, is a preattentive attribute and supports quick comparisons. (Page Laubheimer, "Dashboards: Making Charts and Graphs Easier to Understand", https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/, section "Linear vs. Area-Based Graphs".) Decision: Use a line chart. The light fill under the line is decorative, and values are read from the line's position.
  • Corpus: Dropdowns become a problem above roughly 15 options. In that case a filterable combobox helps. (Huei-Hsin Wang, "Does Your Form Really Need a Dropdown List?", https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dropdown-list/, section "Too Many Options".) Inference/decision: At 14 locations the list is under that threshold, so I used a native <select>, sorted alphabetically to make scanning easier (my inference). With only two dates, a visible two-button toggle replaces a second dropdown (my inference).
  • Corpus: Use larger font sizes for glanceable information, and avoid condensed or thin type for people who are distracted. (Page Laubheimer, "Typography for Glanceable Reading: Bigger Is Better", https://www.nngroup.com/articles/glanceable-fonts/, section "Applying These Findings to Your Designs".) Decision: The current height is about 3.4rem bold system UI type. The trend and next-tide times are bold and 1.25–1.5rem. Someone checking tides on the way to the coast may be distracted (my inference about context).
  • None of these sources is specific to tides or to accessibility. They support general chart, form and typography principles only.

Assumptions

  • Product scope comes from USER_ANSWER.txt ("Proceed as inferred"): someone checking tides for a coastal outing, with no particular activity prioritized. No safety claims and no accounts.
  • "Pacific time" on Oct 6–7, 2026 is PDT (UTC−7). Daylight saving time ends Nov 1, 2026.
  • Fixture hour 24 is midnight starting the next day. It is shown as, for example, "12:00 AM, Thu, Oct 8".
  • The data's trend and nextHigh/nextLow are shown exactly as supplied. For example, Point Reyes on Oct 7 is "Rising" at a 0.44 m low at noon. I did not re-derive them.
  • The tidal datum isn't specified in the data, so no datum is claimed. The demo: true flag is not shown, per the no-banner instruction.
