Corpus facts and design inferences

  • Nielsen Norman Group, Kara Pernice, The Top Enduring Intranet-Design Mistakes: 7 Deadly Sins, section “3. Poor Visual Layout”: type treatment, space and chunking affect hierarchy and scanning. Inference: emphasize current height, group next tides, then show the chart and full-day events. This is a general usability principle applied to coastal checking, not evidence of a tested tide layout.
  • Nielsen Norman Group, Huei-Hsin Wang, Does Your Form Really Need a Dropdown List?, section “Too Few Options”: exposing a handful of choices with radios avoids opening a menu to discover them. Inference: show both dates as native radios with visible selected and focused states.
  • Same article, section “Too Many Options”: roughly more than 15 choices creates scan/scroll cost; filtering can help larger predictable sets. Inference: a labeled native select with 14 alphabetized locations is a reasonable bounded prototype choice. The section does not specifically endorse 14-option selects. Native type-to-select and OS selection UI are retained; a search combobox would merit evaluation if this list grows.
